The MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines got *tighter* on third-party AI between consultation and final text, while easing elsewhere.

§5.10-§5.11 now state the FI's **primary accountability** for its use of third-party AI, require the ability to **limit, suspend or replace** a provider when residual risk exceeds appetite, and accept certifications or independent external assessments - but Response to Feedback §9.9 says a vendor self-attestation unsupported by evidence is generally not an effective way to address a transparency gap. Contractual visibility must cover AI introduction *and updates*, and development controls apply when you fine-tune (fn38).

So the toolkit models a vendor assessment over the eight §5.11 considerations, records whether assurance is independent or self-attested, tracks whether the FI did its own testing, and forces the residual-risk-versus-appetite decision to be written down with a limit/suspend/replace record.

One crosswalk result: §5.11 has no full mapping in ISO/IEC 42001, the NIST AI RMF or the EU AI Act. An ISO 42001 certificate tells you a provider has a management system. It does not test the provider's model behaviour.
