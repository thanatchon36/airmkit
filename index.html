    airmkit¶

    Operationalise the MAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management for LLM and agentic AI systems — and measure how far that is possible.

    Not affiliated with MAS. Not legal or regulatory advice.

    Independent open-source project. Every interpretation and every threshold is the authors'; MAS declines to prescribe materiality formulae, metrics or testing methodologies (Response to Feedback §7.19, §10.28, §10.66). See the disclaimer.

    The Monetary Authority of Singapore issued its Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management on 7 October 2026. Sections 3–4 — oversight, identification, inventory and risk materiality — bind from 7 October 2027; Sections 5–6 — lifecycle controls and capability — from 7 October 2028 (§1.8). The text is thirty pages of principles, and the regulator is explicit that it prescribes no formula, metric or threshold.

    airmkit turns those principles into artefacts an engineer can produce and an auditor can read: a machine-readable clause catalogue, an AI inventory schema with first-class support for agents, a transparent risk-materiality rubric whose every signal cites a paragraph, a control catalogue in which every control cites the paragraph it serves, evidence records that cannot be quietly edited, and an audit pack. The same repository is a pre-registered study of how much of the Guidelines can be machine-checked, with every refutation reported.

    Install and run¶

    pip install airmkit                 # core
pip install "airmkit[evals]"        # adapters for inspect-ai, deepeval, ragas, garak
pip install "airmkit[report,docs]"  # DOCX output and this site

    airm init --institution "Merlion Commercial Bank"   # scaffold a register and rubric
airm scan . --out candidates.yaml                   # find AI already in your code and configs
airm report --register ai-inventory.yaml --out audit-pack/

    Python 3.11+. No GPU, no API key and no network access are needed for the core, and make reproduce rebuilds the whole study on a laptop without a hosted model.

    What the study found¶

    Five questions with thresholds fixed before the experiments ran. Of 17 hypotheses, 10 held and 7 were refuted; all of them are reported with the thresholds they failed.

    Finding
    Automatability 45.1% of expectations admit a fully automated check — 84.6% in Section 4 (inventory, materiality) and 0% in Section 3 (governance). Counted per control, the semi-automatable class dominates.
    Autonomy Moving a use case up a six-rung autonomy ladder adds controls monotonically but rarely moves its tier: tier is dominated by consequence, autonomy shows in the control set.
    Guardrails The patterns that suppress attacks also refuse 50% of benign in-scope requests. No pattern met both pre-registered thresholds; a truncation bug that produced a perfect 0% attack rate is disclosed.
    Crosswalk The ISO 42001 + NIST AI RMF + EU AI Act union leaves 35.3% of expectations with no full mapping — governance machinery and Singapore-specific proportionality, not the agent clauses.
    Cost Evidence volume is flat across tiers; proportionality scales rigour. 93.1% of estimated effort is attestation and documentation.

    The numbers above are quoted from results/summary.json; the paper's threats-to-validity section says what they do and do not support.
